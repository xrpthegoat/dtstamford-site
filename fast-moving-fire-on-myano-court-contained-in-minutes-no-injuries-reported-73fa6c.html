Fast-Moving Fire on Myano Court Contained in Minutes, No Injuries Reported

A fast-moving fire on Myano Court in Stamford was contained within minutes, with no injuries and no damage beyond the unit of origin.

Published · August 10, 2026 at 7:44 AM ET

A fast-moving residential fire on Myano Court in Stamford was quickly contained by firefighters on July 25, 2026, with no injuries reported and the blaze limited to a single unit. For local renters and buyers, this event underscores the importance of fire safety, building maintenance, and the effectiveness of emergency response in densely populated urban areas.

What's happening

On Saturday, July 25, 2026, at approximately 10:40 p.m., Stamford firefighters responded to a structure fire in the 40 block of Myano Court [1]. The fire originated in an exterior deck and rapidly spread to the second floor and attic space of the unit, according to Deputy Fire Chief Matthew Palmer [1]. Despite the fast-moving nature of the blaze, firefighters arrived within four minutes and gained control of the fire within 17 minutes of their arrival [1]. The fire was contained to the unit of origin, preventing damage to adjacent apartments [1]. Stamford Emergency Medical Services and the Stamford Police Department assisted at the scene [1]. The Stamford Fire Marshal’s Office is investigating the cause [1]. No injuries were reported, and the fire did not spread to other units [1].

This incident is distinct from a separate three-alarm fire that occurred on July 19, 2026, at a residential unit on Myano Lane, which involved significant damage and displaced two families [6]. The July 25 fire, however, was reported as being contained more rapidly, with no mention of injuries or displacement in the official report [1].

Fire Safety and Emergency Response in Stamford

The swift containment of the July 25 fire highlights the effectiveness of Stamford’s emergency response infrastructure. The fact that this fire was controlled within 17 minutes of arrival—despite its rapid spread—demonstrates the department’s preparedness and rapid deployment capabilities, especially in high-density residential zones.

The incident also underscores the importance of working smoke alarms and fire-resistant building materials. While the source does not specify the cause of the fire, it does note that the fire was contained to the unit of origin, suggesting that fire barriers and early detection systems may have played a role [1]. The Fire Department has previously emphasized the importance of smoke alarms in preventing fatalities [5], and this event reinforces that message for residents in older or multi-unit buildings.
