Stamford Zoning Board Reviews Proposal for Senior Housing at 35 Terrace Avenue

Stamford’s Zoning Board weighs a six-unit senior housing project amid resident concerns over traffic and privacy.

Uploaded · July 30, 2026 at 12:52 PM ET

A proposal to build six units of senior housing at 35 Terrace Avenue in Stamford’s Bull’s Head neighborhood has drawn public attention and raised concerns among nearby residents, prompting a formal review by the city’s Zoning Board. The project, which aims to provide ADA-compliant, specially adapted housing for seniors or individuals with disabilities, is currently under scrutiny as the board evaluates its impact on neighborhood character, traffic, and privacy. For Stamford renters and buyers, this development reflects broader trends in housing demand, zoning adjustments, and community responses to changing residential needs.

What's happening

The proposed development at 35 Terrace Avenue off High Ridge Road involves six residential units designed to meet Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) standards and follow residential structure guidelines [1]. The project requires a special permit from the Zoning Board, which has scheduled further review and site visits to assess the plan. At a recent Zoning Board meeting, several local residents voiced opposition, citing concerns about increased traffic, reduced privacy due to the homes’ orientation toward neighboring properties, and the perceived mismatch between the project’s scale and the neighborhood’s character [1]. Jaclyn Moeller, a resident on Jessup Street, noted that the street is a “very small through street” and expressed concern about direct sightlines into her home [1]. A petition with 22 signatures was submitted in opposition [1]. James Yaeger and Paula Waldman echoed worries about traffic congestion, with Yaeger stating the area already has “worse” traffic and that six units would not fit this area [1].

In response, the Zoning Board Chairman David Stein advised developers to revise the landscape plan to include fencing and requested renderings of proposed fencing designs [1]. The board also plans a site visit to evaluate the property firsthand. The developer’s attorney, Lenny Braman, argued that the project would benefit the community by addressing the growing need for senior housing in Stamford, noting that the plan met all density requirements in the city's zoning code [1]. A traffic consultant, Wyatt Gerber, stated that the development itself would not generate significant traffic impact, calling it “very negligible.” Gerber acknowledged that traffic can be heavy on nearby High Ridge Road and Long Ridge Road.

Community Response and Zoning Process

The feedback from residents highlights a recurring tension in Stamford’s urban planning: balancing the need for accessible, age-appropriate housing with neighborhood preservation. Residents said the project would change the character and density of their street. While the proposed project is small in scale—only six units—it has sparked debate about how future developments might affect the character of established residential areas. The Zoning Board’s decision will hinge on whether the proposed design, particularly the landscaping and fencing, can mitigate privacy and traffic concerns. As of early April 2026, the board had not yet made a ruling, and further review was scheduled for the Zoning Board’s April 13 meeting.

What this means for Stamford buyers & renters

This development is a microcosm of larger shifts in Stamford’s housing market. As the city continues to grow and age, demand for accessible, adaptable housing is rising—especially in areas where walkability and transit access are strong. The fact that a small-scale senior housing project is being scrutinized so closely reflects how sensitive Stamford residents are to changes in density, especially in quieter, established areas. For renters and buyers, this means that new housing projects, even modest ones, may face delays or require design adjustments to gain approval. That can affect availability and timing, particularly in areas where demand is strong.

For buyers, this underscores the importance of understanding zoning processes and community sentiment when evaluating properties. While the project at 35 Terrace Avenue is small, it signals that the city is actively responding to demographic shifts—particularly the aging population—by allowing more specialized housing. This trend may lead to more such developments in the future, especially in areas with good access to public transit, medical services, and amenities. For renters, this could mean a gradual increase in availability of units tailored to older adults or those with mobility needs. The developer has stated the units would be sold as condos, with leasing only a possibility.

At the same time, the strong resident pushback shows that not all changes are welcomed, especially when they involve visual or traffic impacts. This suggests that future projects will need to be more thoughtful in their design and community engagement. For buyers and renters, it’s a reminder that even small developments can spark significant local debate—and that location, design, and neighborhood dynamics matter deeply when choosing where to live.
