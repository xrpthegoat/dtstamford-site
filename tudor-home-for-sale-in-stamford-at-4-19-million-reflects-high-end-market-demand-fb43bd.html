Tudor Home for Sale in Stamford at $4.19 Million Reflects High-End Market Demand

A $4.19 million Tudor home in Stamford underscores the city’s strong demand for architecturally distinctive, historically significant properties.

Uploaded · August 8, 2026 at 8:09 AM ET

A Tudor-style home in Stamford, Connecticut, is now listed for sale at $4.19 million, highlighting the continued presence of luxury properties in the city’s residential market. For local buyers and renters, this listing underscores the premium pricing and architectural distinctiveness available in certain parts of Stamford, particularly in neighborhoods with historic character and established zoning protections.

What's happening

The property, located in Stamford, is described as a Tudor home featuring timeless architecture and luxurious amenities. It is listed for $4.19 million as of August 2026. While the specific address is not provided in the sources, the listing is consistent with Stamford’s broader zoning framework, which regulates land use, building height, density, and open space requirements. The city’s Zoning Board of Appeals and Planning Board oversee development and permit processes, including site plan reviews and special permits for certain uses. The Zoning Regulations, last updated through February 28, 2026, include a range of district types—from residential (R) to commercial (C) and mixed-use (MX)—each with specific standards for building size, lot coverage, and use. Although the property’s exact zoning district is not specified in the sources, its classification as a historic or architecturally significant home suggests it may fall under a designated residential or architectural review district, which could influence development and renovation standards.

Zoning and Development Context

The City of Stamford’s Zoning Regulations emphasize the protection of public health, safety, and welfare through careful land-use planning. These regulations aim to promote adequate light and air, prevent overcrowding, support transportation infrastructure, and preserve environmental and cultural resources. The city’s zoning system includes both general and special districts, such as the R-D (Designed Residential Districts) and ARD (Architectural Review Design District), which are intended to maintain aesthetic and design integrity in specific neighborhoods. While the sources do not confirm whether this Tudor home is in such a district, the fact that it is being marketed as a property with “timeless architecture” suggests it may be subject to stricter design standards or require special approvals for alterations. The Planning Board and Zoning Board of Appeals are responsible for reviewing applications and appeals related to development, including variances and special permits.

What this means for Stamford buyers & renters

This $4.19 million listing is not just a transaction—it’s a signal of how the Stamford housing market continues to reward architectural distinction and historical character. For buyers, it reflects a strong demand for homes that stand out from the standard suburban or modern apartment-style offerings. In a city where new construction is often constrained by zoning and infrastructure capacity, existing homes with unique features—like the steeply pitched roofs, stone detailing, and asymmetrical facades typical of Tudor design—hold significant value. These homes are not just investments; they are rare assets in a market where new builds are increasingly limited by regulatory and logistical challenges.

For renters, this listing underscores the widening gap between rental availability and affordability. While the property is not for rent, its price point indicates that the most desirable homes in Stamford—those in well-maintained, historically significant areas—are increasingly out of reach for typical renters. The city’s zoning regulations, which prioritize light, air, and open space, also limit the density of new construction, keeping supply constrained. As a result, rental prices in high-demand neighborhoods continue to rise, especially for units with character, proximity to transit, and walkability.

The presence of such a high-end listing also suggests that Stamford remains a magnet for buyers seeking both urban convenience and architectural authenticity. With strong transit access to New York City via Metro-North and growing walkability in downtown and near the waterfront, the city continues to attract professionals and families who value location and quality. However, the high price of homes like this Tudor property means that affordability remains a persistent challenge, particularly for first-time buyers and renters.

Ultimately, this listing is not an isolated event—it’s part of a broader trend in Stamford’s real estate market. The city’s zoning framework, while designed to protect community values, also contributes to a limited supply of new housing. This scarcity, combined with strong demand, continues to drive up prices and reinforce the premium placed on homes with enduring design and historical significance.
