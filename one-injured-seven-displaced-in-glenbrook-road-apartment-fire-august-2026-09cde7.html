One Injured, Seven Displaced in Glenbrook Road Apartment Fire, August 2026

A kitchen fire in a Stamford apartment building left one person hospitalized and seven displaced.

Uploaded · August 5, 2026 at 8:04 AM ET

A residential fire on Glenbrook Road in Stamford occurred on Friday, August 2026, around 9:30 p.m., resulting in one person being hospitalized and seven residents temporarily displaced. The blaze originated in the kitchen of a rear basement unit in the 400 block of Glenbrook Road. Emergency crews responded quickly, containing the fire and preventing further spread. The injured individual suffered possible smoke inhalation and was transported to a local hospital. The Red Cross is providing support to the displaced families. The cause of the fire remains under investigation by the Stamford Fire Marshal’s Office.

What's happening

The incident occurred on Friday, July 31, 2026, at approximately 9:30 p.m. in a multi-unit residential building located in the 400 block of Glenbrook Road. The fire began in the kitchen of a rear basement apartment. Emergency services, including the Stamford Fire Department, Stamford Police, and Stamford EMS, responded promptly. The fire was contained within a short time, and no additional injuries to civilians or firefighters were reported. One occupant of the fire apartment was transported to Stamford Hospital with smoke-inhalation symptoms; other occupants exited the building without reported injury. The apartment where the fire originated sustained significant damage. The Red Cross is assisting the displaced residents with immediate needs. The Stamford Fire Marshal’s Office is investigating the cause of the fire. No specific details about the building’s age, construction type, or safety systems were provided in the sources.

Fire safety and community response

The swift response by multiple emergency units—including fire, police, and medical services—helped limit the damage and ensure the safety of all occupants. One resident was transported to Stamford Hospital with smoke-inhalation symptoms, but no other injuries were reported among residents or emergency personnel. This outcome underscores the importance of working smoke detectors and evacuation plans. The involvement of the Red Cross underscores the community’s preparedness for such emergencies and the availability of immediate support for displaced individuals. The fire’s origin in a kitchen, a common source of residential fires, reinforces the need for regular appliance maintenance, awareness of cooking hazards, and the presence of working smoke alarms in every unit.

What this means for Stamford buyers & renters

This incident, while isolated, serves as a reminder of the importance of fire safety in any residential property—especially in older buildings or those with shared walls. For renters in Stamford, it underscores the value of knowing your building’s evacuation routes, checking smoke detectors regularly, and having a personal emergency plan. For buyers, it reinforces the need to conduct thorough due diligence, including reviewing building inspection reports, understanding the age and condition of the structure, and verifying the presence of updated fire safety systems such as sprinklers, alarms, and fire doors. While this fire was contained and caused no fatalities, it highlights that even well-managed buildings can experience unexpected events. In a market like Stamford’s—where demand for housing remains strong and rental prices are elevated—buyers and renters should prioritize properties with modern safety features and a proven track record of maintenance. The city’s proximity to New York City, strong public transit access, and walkable neighborhoods continue to drive interest, but safety and reliability remain key factors in long-term housing decisions. This event reminds us that while Stamford offers a vibrant urban lifestyle, vigilance and preparedness are essential for anyone living in a dense, multi-unit environment.
