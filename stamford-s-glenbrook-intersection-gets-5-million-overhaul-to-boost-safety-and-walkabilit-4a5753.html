Stamford’s Glenbrook Intersection Gets $5 Million Overhaul to Boost Safety and Walkability

Stamford’s Glenbrook intersection gets a $5M overhaul to boost safety, walkability, and transit access with new sidewalks, crosswalks, and lighting.

Uploaded · August 5, 2026 at 4:43 PM ET

Stamford residents and commuters should take note: a major redesign of the Glenbrook Road and Church Street intersection is moving forward with a $5 million investment, aiming to improve safety, pedestrian access, and transit connectivity. For buyers and renters in the area, this project signals a long-term commitment to enhancing neighborhood livability—especially for those who rely on walking, biking, or public transit. The upgrades, expected to begin in 2027, will reshape one of the city’s key crossroads in the Glenbrook neighborhood.

What's happening

The City of Stamford is undertaking a comprehensive redesign of the intersection at Glenbrook Road and Church Street, located in the Glenbrook neighborhood of Stamford, Connecticut. The project, funded by a grant from the Connecticut Department of Transportation’s Local Transportation Capital Improvement Program (LOTCIP), will cost just over $5 million. As of August 2026, 60% of the design work is complete, and construction is anticipated to start in 2027.

Key improvements include:

  • A reconfigured intersection to improve safety and traffic operations.
  • Installation of new traffic signals at Glenbrook Road and Church Street, as well as at Crescent Street, both compliant with the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA).
  • Replacement of deteriorating sidewalks along Glenbrook Road from Crescent Street to Church Street, and from Cowing Place to Parker Avenue, with new ADA-compliant sidewalks.
  • Addition of raised crosswalks and sidewalk bump-outs to shorten pedestrian crossing distances and improve visibility.
  • New roadway and pedestrian lighting.
  • Realignment of the Kirkham Place intersection and the creation of a new green space.
  • Improved pedestrian access to the Metro-North Glenbrook station.

The project stems from a 2015 transit-oriented development (TOD) feasibility study that identified the intersection’s outdated infrastructure and safety concerns. The redesign supports Stamford’s Vision Zero initiative, launched in 2022, which aims to eliminate all traffic-related fatalities by 2032. Senior Transportation Planner Luke Buttenwieser emphasized that the upgrades are intended to make the area more pedestrian-friendly and to improve access to the Glenbrook train station.

While construction will involve some road closures, officials have confirmed that pedestrian access to local businesses will be maintained throughout the project. Utility pole relocations are also planned as part of the work.

Public engagement and project timeline

The city has held multiple public meetings to inform residents, including a virtual session in July 2026. Written comments can be submitted to Luke Buttenwieser at the City of Stamford’s Transportation, Traffic & Parking Department, 888 Washington Blvd.

What this means for Stamford buyers & renters

As a real estate agent in Stamford, I see this project not just as a traffic improvement, but as a meaningful signal of the city’s ongoing investment in walkable, transit-connected neighborhoods—areas that are increasingly in demand among homebuyers and renters alike.

The Glenbrook intersection sits at a critical juncture between residential, commercial, and transit infrastructure. Upgrades like ADA-compliant sidewalks, raised crosswalks, and better lighting directly enhance the daily experience of walking, especially for older adults, children, and people with disabilities. In a city where many residents rely on walking to the train station, even small improvements to safety and comfort can significantly increase the perceived value of nearby housing.

For renters, this means a more reliable, pleasant commute—particularly for those who use the Glenbrook station. As Stamford continues to attract professionals seeking urban convenience without the high cost of downtown Manhattan, neighborhoods like Glenbrook are becoming more competitive. The project reinforces the idea that this area is being upgraded for long-term livability, not just short-term convenience.

For buyers, this kind of infrastructure investment often correlates with stable or rising property values over time. While the full impact won’t be visible until after construction, the fact that the city is dedicating $5 million to this intersection suggests a strategic focus on improving access and safety in a high-traffic, transit-accessible corridor. That kind of commitment tends to attract buyers who prioritize walkability, safety, and future-proofing their investment.

It’s also worth noting that the project aligns with broader trends in Stamford’s real estate market: increasing demand for homes within a 10-minute walk of transit, especially those with access to Metro-North. As the city continues to invest in infrastructure that supports active transportation and reduces car dependency, neighborhoods that are already walkable—like Glenbrook—are likely to see sustained or growing interest.

While the project won’t immediately change rental prices or home values, it’s a strong indicator that the city is prioritizing quality of life in areas beyond downtown. For anyone considering a move to Stamford, especially those looking to minimize car use or commute by train, this intersection upgrade is a tangible sign that the city is building a more connected, accessible, and sustainable future.
