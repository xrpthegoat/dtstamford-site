Stamford vs. Greenwich

Two of Fairfield County's marquee addresses, ten minutes apart — but they feel like different worlds. Prices, taxes, commute, schools, and neighborhoods: the honest comparison to help you choose.

Last updated · August 2026

I show homes in both towns, sometimes in the same afternoon, and here's the honest headline: a comparable single-family home typically runs $1M–$1.5M less in Stamford than in Greenwich, while Greenwich answers back with a property tax rate roughly half of Stamford's and public schools that are consistently ranked among Connecticut's best. Both towns sit on the same Metro-North express line to Grand Central, ten minutes apart on I-95 — and they feel like different worlds. This is the full comparison I walk my own buyers through: prices, taxes, commute, schools, lifestyle, and which neighborhoods actually map to each other.

The short verdict

Pick Stamford if…

You want the most home for your money, a walkable downtown with real nightlife, a condo or rental market that actually exists, the best train service on the New Haven Line — or you're a first-time buyer, NYC transplant, or downsizer who values energy over acreage.

Pick Greenwich if…

Budget is genuinely secondary and you want estate-level space and privacy, a uniformly top-rated public school district, one of Connecticut's lowest property tax rates, and a polished, established town with brand-name prestige.

Price: the biggest difference

This is where the two towns split hardest, so let's start here. Greenwich is one of the most expensive towns in America. As a mid-2025 benchmark, its median single-family sale price exceeded $2,500,000, and the luxury end runs into the tens of millions (SmartMLS/Redfin, mid-2025). Stamford spans a much wider range — the median single-family sale price ran in the $800,000–$1,000,000 range over the same period, condos start in the mid-$300s, and single-family entry points begin in the high $400s. That condo-and-starter segment barely exists in Greenwich at all.

Put plainly: a comparable single-family home typically runs $1M–$1.5M less in Stamford. That's not a rounding error — it's the difference between buying now and waiting a decade, or between a mortgage that lets you live and one that owns you. If you want the most home (or any condo at all) for your money, Stamford usually wins. If you want to see where the numbers sit this month, my Stamford market page tracks live data, and if you're still weighing whether to buy at all, run the math on my renting vs. buying breakdown first.

Property taxes: the mill-rate trap

Here's the one that surprises people in both directions. Connecticut towns tax you on 70% of assessed market value times a "mill rate" — and the two towns' rates are wildly different. Stamford's mill rate is roughly 23 for most of the city (District A 23.36; districts range 22.76–23.36, FY 2024–25). Greenwich runs roughly 11–12 mills — one of the lowest rates in the state, because its enormous grand list of high-value homes spreads the town budget thin.

Back-of-the-envelope on the same $800,000 house: in Stamford you'd pay roughly $13,000 a year; in Greenwich, roughly $6,500. That's a real gap. But here's the trap in both directions: you almost never buy the same-priced house. The Greenwich home that competes with an $800K Stamford colonial costs $2M+, so the absolute dollars out the door in Greenwich are usually higher even at half the rate. Compare the bill, not the rate — and verify current figures with each town's assessor before you budget, because rates reset with revaluations. I break down how Stamford's districts and the 2027 revaluation work in my Stamford property taxes guide.

The commute to Grand Central

Near tie — and I say that as someone who hears "but Greenwich is closer" weekly. Both towns sit on Metro-North's New Haven Line. Greenwich is one stop closer to the city. Stamford is the biggest express hub on the line: express trains reach Grand Central in about 45–50 minutes, frequency is the best in Connecticut, and Stamford adds Amtrak service Greenwich doesn't have. Plenty of express trains blow straight past Greenwich, so in practice a Stamford commuter often gets to their desk just as fast.

For a daily commuter, the frequency difference matters more than the few minutes of track. Missing a train in Stamford costs you fifteen minutes; missing one in a smaller station's schedule can cost you forty. I've broken down every scenario — express vs. local, parking, monthly costs — in my Stamford-to-NYC commute guide, and if you're leaving the city altogether, start with moving from NYC to Stamford.

Schools: Greenwich's strongest card

Let me be straight, because pretending otherwise would insult your intelligence: Greenwich public schools are consistently ranked among the top districts in Connecticut, they're well funded, and for many families that alone settles the debate. If you want one uniformly strong district where any school your kid lands in is excellent, Greenwich delivers that.

Stamford's answer is options rather than uniformity. It runs a larger, more diverse district with a wide range of schools, plus magnet schools, IB programs, and strong private alternatives. Some Stamford schools compete with anything in the county; others don't — which is why I tell every family the same thing: compare school by school, not town by town. Districts are averages; your kid attends one building. Tell me your priorities and I'll point you at the Stamford neighborhoods whose schools actually fit them — the neighborhoods guide is the place to start.

Lifestyle: small city vs. established town

This is the factor people underweight on a spreadsheet and feel every single day. Stamford is a small city. Downtown has high-rises, a genuine restaurant-and-bar scene on Bedford Street, a movie theater, concerts at the ballpark, and sidewalks that actually take you somewhere. Harbor Point adds a waterfront boardwalk lined with new buildings and marinas. The energy skews younger and more urban — you can live here without touching your car all weekend.

Greenwich is a polished, established town. Greenwich Avenue is one of the best shopping-and-dining streets in the Northeast, the country clubs are the real thing, and the backcountry is sprawling estates behind stone walls. The atmosphere is quieter, calmer, more private — old money that doesn't need to announce itself. Nights end earlier; that's a feature, not a bug, for the people who choose it.

My shorthand after years of showing both: want sidewalks, restaurants, and buzz? Stamford. Want estates, clubs, and calm? Greenwich. Neither answer is wrong — they're just different lives.

Waterfront: boardwalk vs. beach club

Both towns live on Long Island Sound, differently. Stamford's waterfront is public-facing and urban: the Harbor Point boardwalk, Cove Island Park's beaches and trails, Cummings and West Beach, kayak launches, and marina slips a normal person can rent. Greenwich's waterfront is more private and more spectacular: Greenwich Point (Tod's Point) is a genuinely beautiful town park, but much of the shoreline belongs to yacht clubs, beach associations, and waterfront estates. If waterfront living for you means a condo balcony over the marina, that's Stamford. If it means a lawn running down to your own dock, that's Greenwich — priced accordingly.

Renting first? That decides it

If you're not buying yet, the comparison is short. Stamford has one of the deepest rental markets in Connecticut — thousands of professionally managed apartments downtown and at Harbor Point, plus a broad condo-rental layer underneath. Greenwich rental stock is thin: a handful of complexes, accessory apartments, and the occasional estate cottage. Nearly everyone who "tries the area before buying" rents in Stamford first — often while shopping both towns. That's a strategy I actively recommend, and the rent-vs-buy math will tell you when to make the switch.

How the two markets behave

One more difference that matters when you're actually writing offers: the two markets move at different speeds. Stamford's mid-market is fast. Well-priced single-family homes in the $500K–$900K band routinely draw multiple offers within the first weekend, because that price point serves the deepest pool of buyers in Fairfield County — locals, NYC transplants, and investors all at once. Condos move briskly too, though with more room to negotiate. Greenwich is slower and more deliberate. Estate-level properties can sit for months waiting for the right buyer, price cuts are common at the top end, and negotiation is expected — but the trophy streets in Old Greenwich and Riverside can be every bit as competitive as Stamford's starter market. Practically: budget for speed in Stamford and patience in Greenwich, and get your financing fully underwritten either way. I keep current pace-of-market numbers on the Stamford market page.

Which neighborhoods actually compare

"Stamford vs. Greenwich" is really a set of neighborhood-level matchups. Here's the mapping I use with buyers who love a Greenwich look but want a Stamford price:

  • North Stamford ↔ backcountry Greenwich. Acreage, privacy, winding roads, and mid-century-to-colonial stock — the closest thing to the backcountry feel at a fraction of the price.
  • Shippan ↔ Old Greenwich / Riverside. A peninsula of waterfront and water-view streets with a beach-community rhythm — Stamford's answer to the OG lifestyle.
  • Harbor Point ↔ Old Greenwich, for a different buyer. Both are "live near the water, walk to dinner" — but Harbor Point does it with new-construction condos and rentals on a boardwalk instead of village colonials.
  • Downtown Stamford ↔ central Greenwich. Walk-to-everything living near the train — high-rise condos on one side, Avenue-adjacent townhomes at several times the price on the other.
  • Springdale & Glenbrook ↔ Cos Cob. Village centers with their own train stations and the most approachable single-family price points in each town.
  • The Cove ↔ Byram. Each town's under-the-radar waterfront value pocket, beach included.

Browse all of them in the Stamford neighborhoods guide — or tell me which Greenwich street you fell for and I'll show you its Stamford cousin.

Who tends to choose which

Stamford draws first-time buyers, renters, condo buyers, NYC transplants who want city energy without Manhattan prices, and downsizers who want walkability. Greenwich draws established high earners and families after estate-style homes, top schools, space, and privacy, with budget a secondary concern.

And the traffic runs both ways: empty-nesters trade Greenwich estates for Stamford condos every season, while growing Stamford families trade walkability for a Greenwich yard and school district. These towns are neighbors, not rivals — in my experience the choice tracks life stage far more than taste.

The bottom line

If budget is a real factor, you want walkable city living, you're buying a first home or a condo, or you're renting before you buy — Stamford is usually the smarter move, and not as a consolation prize. If you're after a prestige estate, maximum space and privacy, a uniformly top public school district, and the tax rate to sweeten it — Greenwich delivers that like almost nowhere else in the country. Frequently Asked Questions

Is Stamford or Greenwich more affordable?

Stamford, by a wide margin. As a mid-2025 benchmark, Stamford's median single-family sale price ran in the $800,000–$1,000,000 range while Greenwich's exceeded $2,500,000 (SmartMLS/Redfin). Stamford also has condos starting in the mid-$300s — a segment that barely exists in Greenwich — so there's a real entry point at almost every budget.

Why are property taxes lower in Greenwich than in Stamford?

Greenwich runs one of the lowest mill rates in Connecticut — roughly 11–12 mills versus Stamford's roughly 23 (FY 2024–25) — because its huge base of high-value homes spreads the town budget thin. On the same-priced house, Greenwich's annual bill is roughly half of Stamford's. But Greenwich homes cost far more, so the total dollars you actually pay there are usually higher. Verify current rates with each town's assessor.

Which town has a better commute to Manhattan?

Near tie. Both are on Metro-North's New Haven Line. Greenwich is one stop closer, but Stamford is the biggest express hub on the line — express trains reach Grand Central in about 45–50 minutes, with the best frequency in Connecticut plus Amtrak service. For most daily commuters, Stamford's frequency matters more than Greenwich's few saved minutes.

Is Stamford or Greenwich better for families?

Greenwich is consistently ranked among Connecticut's top public school districts and suits families who want one uniformly strong system with abundant space. Stamford runs a larger, more diverse district with magnet schools, IB programs, and strong private alternatives, alongside urban amenities. Compare school by school rather than town-wide — happy to point you to the right Stamford neighborhoods for your priorities.

Can I rent in Greenwich the way I can in Stamford?

Not really. Stamford has thousands of professionally managed apartments — especially downtown and at Harbor Point — plus a deep condo-rental market. Greenwich rental stock is thin, skewing to a few complexes and accessory units. Most people who want to try the area before buying rent in Stamford first, even when they're shopping both towns.

Which Stamford neighborhoods feel most like Greenwich?

North Stamford is the closest match to backcountry Greenwich — acreage, privacy, and winding roads at a fraction of the price. Shippan echoes Old Greenwich and Riverside's waterfront feel, while Springdale and Glenbrook offer village-and-train-station living similar to Cos Cob.

Do people move from Greenwich to Stamford?

Yes, all the time — most often empty-nesters trading an estate for a walkable Stamford condo. The reverse move, Stamford to Greenwich, is common for growing families chasing space and the school district. The two towns are neighbors, not rivals; the choice usually tracks life stage more than preference.

Is Stamford a consolation prize if I'm priced out of Greenwich?

No. Buyers priced out of Greenwich, Darien, New Canaan, or Westport routinely discover Stamford is the answer, not the fallback — more housing types, a real nightlife and restaurant scene, a public waterfront, and the best train service on the New Haven Line.
