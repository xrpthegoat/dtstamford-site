Stamford Schools Appoint New Assistant Superintendent Amid Leadership Transition

Stamford schools appoint Scott Clayton as assistant superintendent, ensuring stability in secondary education amid enrollment changes.

Uploaded · July 31, 2026

The Stamford Board of Education has named Scott Clayton as the new assistant superintendent for secondary schools, effective immediately, following the abrupt departure of Matthew Laskowski, who accepted a principal role in Greenwich just days after being hired [1]. This leadership change comes at a critical time for the district, as it continues to navigate shifting enrollment trends and evolving educational priorities.

What's happening

On July 14, 2026, the Stamford Board of Education unanimously approved Scott Clayton for the assistant superintendent position overseeing middle schools, high schools, and adult and continuing education programs [1]. Clayton, who has served as the district’s director of student data and systems since 2024, brings a deep institutional background to the role. He previously led Scofield Magnet Middle School for 12 years and held leadership roles at Newtown High School, including assistant principal and dean of students [1]. His academic credentials include a bachelor’s in history from Western Connecticut State University, a master’s in secondary education, a sixth-year certificate in educational leadership from Sacred Heart University, and superintendent certification from Central Connecticut State University [1].

The vacancy arose after Matthew Laskowski was initially appointed on June 23, 2026, but declined the position to accept a principal role at Western Middle School in Greenwich [1]. The board moved swiftly to fill the gap, emphasizing Clayton’s proven track record in instructional leadership, operational management, and district-wide data systems [1]. Superintendent Adrian Talley praised Clayton for his “commitment to student achievement, continuous improvement, and collaboration,” noting his success as both a principal and a central office leader [1].

Stamford’s Educational and Community Context

The appointment comes amid broader shifts in Stamford’s public education landscape. In early 2026, the district reported a significant drop in overall student enrollment—down 891 students from the prior year, marking the lowest count since 2009 [6]. A particularly sharp decline was seen in the New Arrivals program for immigrant students, which saw a 60% reduction, from 132 to 52 students [6]. District officials cited national immigration policies and political climate as contributing factors, though no official data links this trend directly to the leadership change [6].

Meanwhile, the city’s long-term planning framework, the Stamford 2035 Comprehensive Plan, was formally approved in September 2025 after months of public input [2]. This plan guides future development across areas including sustainability, housing, economic development, mobility, infrastructure, and community wellbeing, without altering existing zoning regulations [2]. It serves as a strategic vision for growth, influencing how land use, transportation, and public services evolve through 2035 [2]. While the plan does not mandate specific changes, it shapes how future projects are evaluated and prioritized [2].

What this means for Stamford buyers & renters

For Stamford renters and buyers, this leadership appointment signals stability and continuity in one of the city’s most vital public institutions. The swift replacement of a key education leader after a short vacancy reflects strong governance and a commitment to maintaining operational momentum—especially important in a city where school quality remains a major factor in housing demand.

The fact that Clayton is a long-time Stamford educator with deep roots in the district’s middle and high school systems suggests a focus on internal development and data-driven improvement. This kind of leadership often correlates with consistent, incremental progress in school performance and student support—factors that can influence neighborhood desirability over time. While no new school construction or major facility changes are mentioned in the sources, the emphasis on data systems and student achievement indicates a strategic investment in existing infrastructure.

For renters, especially those with school-aged children, this stability may offer reassurance. A consistent leadership team can mean more predictable academic environments, which is valuable for families seeking reliable schooling options. For buyers, particularly those in older neighborhoods or near schools, the appointment reinforces the idea that Stamford’s public education system remains a core asset, even amid broader demographic shifts like the recent enrollment decline [6].

The city’s ongoing focus on the 2035 Comprehensive Plan [2] also suggests that future development will be guided by long-term goals around sustainability, walkability, and community wellbeing—values that align with the preferences of many urban buyers and renters. While no specific projects are named, the plan’s emphasis on mixed-use development, transit access, and neighborhood preservation supports a housing market that values proximity to downtown, public transit, and walkable amenities.

In short, this leadership change is not a disruption but a reaffirmation of institutional strength. For those considering a move to Stamford, it underscores that the city’s core services—especially education—are being managed with continuity and long-term vision. Whether you're renting a downtown apartment or buying a home in a residential neighborhood, this stability contributes to a more predictable and resilient local market.
