Stamford Residents Warned of 'Gold Courier' Scam Targeting Seniors

Stamford residents are urged to stay alert as a deceptive 'gold courier' scam targets seniors with false claims of financial danger.

Published · July 15, 2026 at 7:53 AM ET

Stamford police have issued a public alert about a growing scam that preys on senior citizens, urging residents to remain vigilant. The scam, known as the "gold courier" scheme, involves fraudsters convincing victims to liquidate savings or investments into cash or precious metals—often gold or silver—under the false pretense that their financial assets are at risk. These criminals then arrange for couriers to collect the physical assets, effectively stealing the victim’s wealth. The warning comes as part of a broader national trend, with over $300 million in losses reported across the U.S. in 2025 alone from similar scams [2].

What's happening

The Stamford Police Financial Crimes Unit is actively warning residents that scammers are impersonating government officials, banks, or tech support representatives to gain trust [2]. Victims are told their accounts are compromised or under threat, and that the only way to protect their money is to convert it into physical cash or gold and deliver it to a courier. No legitimate government or financial institution would ever request such a transfer [2]. The scam is especially targeting older adults, who may be less familiar with modern fraud tactics or more trusting of authority figures. Police emphasize that anyone receiving such a call or message should hang up immediately and report the incident to local law enforcement or the Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) [2].

The alert was first issued on July 14, 2026, by local news outlets including the *Newport Dispatch* and *Patch*, citing the Stamford Police Department [2][3]. While the sources confirm the nature and scope of the scam, they do not provide specific details about how many Stamford residents have been affected, nor do they list any known scammer identities, addresses, or specific delivery locations within the city [2][3]. The exact number of victims in Stamford remains unreported, and no arrests or prosecutions related to this specific scam have been publicly confirmed as of July 20, 2026.

How to protect yourself

Residents are advised to be cautious of unsolicited calls or messages claiming to be from government agencies, banks, or tech companies. If a caller demands immediate action—especially involving cash, gold, or delivery of valuables—this is a red flag. Never transfer money or physical assets to unknown individuals, even if they claim to be “couriers” or “official representatives.” It is critical to verify the legitimacy of any such request through independent channels, such as calling the organization directly using a known phone number, not one provided by the caller.

Common questions

How do 'gold courier' scams typically work?

Scammers impersonate officials, claiming your assets are at risk, then pressure you to convert savings into cash or gold and deliver it to a courier—no legitimate institution would ever request this.

What should I do if I receive a suspicious call about my finances?

Hang up immediately. Do not provide information or transfer assets. Verify the caller’s identity through official channels using known contact details, not numbers provided during the call.
