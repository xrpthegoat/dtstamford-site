How Much Does It Cost to Buy a House in Stamford, CT?

At Stamford's sold median of $727,000, plan on $55,000 to $190,000 in cash to close — a 5%–20% down payment plus 2%–5% in closing costs. The rest is your mortgage. Below is every cost broken out, including the ones buyers forget.

Last updated · July 2026

"What do I actually need to buy here?" is the first question most Stamford buyers ask. The purchase price is only part of it. Here's the honest breakdown of the cash you need upfront and what you'll carry monthly — and where the real number depends on your loan and the specific home.

Short answer: Budget three upfront buckets — down payment, closing costs (usually a few percent of price), and moving/move-in — plus ongoing Stamford property taxes. Connecticut buyers don't pay the state conveyance tax (that's the seller's), but you do pay your own attorney. Get pre-approved to see your real numbers.

What are the upfront costs to buy a house in Stamford, CT?

Budget three main buckets: a down payment (typically 3%–20%+ of the purchase price), closing costs (usually 2–4% of the price), and inspection and moving expenses.

Cost Item What It Covers Typical Range
Down payment Your equity stake at purchase — more down means a smaller loan and no PMI at 20%+ 3%–20%+ of price (depends on loan program)
Closing costs Lender/origination fees, attorney (CT is attorney-closing), title insurance, recording fees, and prepaids (taxes + insurance into escrow) ~2%–4% of price
Inspections General home inspection + specialty checks you choose (radon, oil tank, sewer, pest) $400–$1,500+
Moving & move-in Movers, immediate repairs, and initial setup Varies

Connecticut note: the state and municipal conveyance tax is a seller cost, not a buyer cost — so that transfer tax isn't on your side of the table.

What are the property taxes in Stamford, CT?

Your yearly property tax is the home's assessed value times Stamford's current mill rate. Because rates and assessments change, I won't post a number that goes stale — for any home you're considering, I'll pull the current assessment and rate so you see the real monthly figure before you commit. Factor in homeowner's insurance and, if applicable, HOA/condo fees.

Do I need an attorney?

Yes — Connecticut closings are handled by real-estate attorneys, and that fee is part of your closing costs. It's standard here; I can refer experienced local closing attorneys.

What should I do first before buying a home in Stamford?

Before you fall for a listing, get a lender pre-approval. It turns all of the above into real dollars for your situation, tells you your comfortable price range, and makes your offer credible in a competitive Stamford market. I work with local lenders and can connect you.
