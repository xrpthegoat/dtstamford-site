Community Family Give Back Day Returns to Stamford with Free Resources and Family Activities

Stamford hosts its annual Community Family Give Back Day with free supplies, health screenings, and activities for all ages.

Uploaded · August 16, 2026 at 7:41 AM ET

Stamford residents seeking free back-to-school supplies, health screenings, and community engagement now have a well-established annual event to attend. The Community Family Give Back Day, hosted in Stamford, offers a no-cost, family-friendly day of services and activities each summer. For renters and buyers alike, this event reflects ongoing community investment in accessible resources—key for anyone evaluating the city’s quality of life and neighborhood vitality.

What's happening

The Community Family Give Back Day is a recurring free event organized in partnership with the Community Action Agency of Western Connecticut, Power Health, and Organized Youth. The event is co-sponsored by BLT, Old Navy, and Staples.

The 2024 event took place on Saturday, August 17, from 12:00 PM to 4:00 PM at the Lathon Wider Community Center, 34 Woodland Ave, Stamford, CT 06902.

The event features a range of services and activities, including:

  • Book bag and school supply giveaways (first come, first served)
  • Health and wellness information and screenings
  • Kids’ activities and live DJ entertainment
  • Pizza and refreshments
  • Community resource booths and gift bags

The 2025 event occurred on Saturday, August 16, from 12:00 PM to 4:00 PM at the University of Connecticut-Stamford Campus, 1 University Place, Stamford, CT 06901, with programming including book giveaways, health screenings, and a live DJ.

The 2026 Community Family Give Back Day took place on Saturday, August 15, 2026, from 12:00 PM to 4:00 PM at the University of Connecticut-Stamford Campus, 1 University Place, Stamford, CT 06901, according to the official Eventbrite listing by organizer Power Health Tour, corroborated by Hey Stamford! and UNATION.

What this means for Stamford buyers & renters

As a local real estate agent in Stamford, I see events like the Community Family Give Back Day not just as one-day happenings, but as indicators of deeper neighborhood health and civic engagement. The fact that this event continues annually—especially in high-visibility downtown locations like Woodland Avenue and the UConn campus—shows that Stamford is actively supporting access to basic needs through public-private partnerships. For renters and buyers, this kind of consistent programming signals a city that values inclusivity and resident well-being, which can influence long-term neighborhood stability.

Downtown Stamford, where the event has been held, remains one of the most transit-accessible areas in the region, with easy connections to Metro-North, the Stamford Transit Center, and major employers. Events like this one enhance walkability and public use of shared spaces, reinforcing the area’s appeal. For renters, especially those with children, access to free school supplies and health screenings reduces household costs and increases the perceived value of living in a walkable, service-rich neighborhood. For buyers, proximity to such community resources can be a subtle but meaningful factor in long-term property value, particularly in a market where amenities and quality of life are increasingly competitive.

Moreover, the event’s recurring nature—held at the Lathon Wider Community Center in 2024 and at the UConn Stamford Campus in 2025 and 2026—reflects a consistent pattern of community collaboration and access to essential services. This kind of sustained effort often correlates with lower vacancy rates and stronger tenant retention in nearby rental buildings. It also reflects a broader trend in Stamford toward integrating social services into public spaces, making the city more attractive to young professionals, families, and individuals seeking community connection without sacrificing convenience.

While the exact 2027 date and location have not yet been announced, the event’s persistence underscores a cultural commitment to accessibility and inclusion. For anyone considering a move to Stamford—whether to rent or buy—this kind of consistent, no-cost community support is a tangible sign of a city that invests in its residents. It’s not just about the free backpacks or pizza; it’s about the signal it sends: that Stamford is a place where basic needs are met, and where people are encouraged to participate in shared spaces.
