Stamford Waterfront Home with Club Access Lists for $2.2 Million

Stamford’s first waterfront listing in nearly 50 years offers private club access, direct Sound views, and resort-style amenities in a rare, low-density enclave.

Uploaded · July 30, 2026 at 4:59 PM ET

A custom-built home in Stamford’s exclusive Dolphin Cove neighborhood is now on the market for $2.2 million, marking the first time in nearly 50 years that 2 Joshua Slocum Dock has been offered for sale. With three bedrooms, three bathrooms, and direct access to a private club and Long Island Sound, the property appeals to buyers seeking a rare blend of waterfront living and resort-style amenities. For local renters and buyers, this listing highlights the ongoing demand for premium, low-density homes in a highly sought-after coastal enclave.

What's happening

The home at 2 Joshua Slocum Dock, built in 1978, is listed for $2.2 million and spans 2,615 square feet. It is located in the private Dolphin Cove community, a waterfront neighborhood in Stamford, Connecticut, known for its deep-water access and exclusive amenities. The property is described as a “gem” that has been “lovingly cared for by its original owners for nearly 5 decades.”

Dolphin Cove offers residents access to a 3-acre club area directly on Long Island Sound, which includes two swimming pools, a full bar, a large pavilion for events, two private beaches, a tennis court, and boat storage racks. The community is noted for its consistent water depth—sailboats with drafts up to seven feet can navigate the man-made waterways even at low tide, thanks to the absence of silting from rivers.

The home is listed as a Cape Cod-style residence with three bedrooms and three bathrooms, and is currently the only active listing in the Dolphin Cove neighborhood. It is being marketed by The Riverside Realty Group. The property was listed in July 2026, and no homes in the area have sold in the past 12 months, according to the community’s listing platform.

The neighborhood and access

Dolphin Cove is situated along the Long Island Sound, with views of Stamford Harbor and the Old Greenwich shoreline. The community is governed by the Davenport Point Waterfront Association, which oversees shared amenities and maintenance. The neighborhood’s street names—such as Flying Cloud Road, Dolphin Cove Quay, and Joshua Slocum Dock—reflect a nautical theme, reinforcing its maritime character.

While the property is private and not open to the public, the club grounds are accessible to residents and their guests. The community’s deep-water access is a key feature, allowing for year-round boating regardless of tidal conditions. This infrastructure is maintained through the association, with minimal need for dredging due to the man-made nature of the waterways.

What this means for Stamford buyers & renters

This listing is a clear signal of how limited and valuable waterfront living remains in Stamford, even in a market where inventory is tight and prices are elevated. The fact that this home has been owned by the same family for nearly 50 years underscores the rarity of such opportunities—especially in a private, amenity-rich neighborhood like Dolphin Cove. For buyers, this is a rare chance to step into a long-established community with a proven lifestyle, rather than a new development with uncertain long-term appeal.

For renters, this listing reinforces the idea that homes in private club communities like Dolphin Cove rarely come up for rent, so this kind of waterfront living is typically a purchase market. The $2.2 million price point places the home firmly in the luxury segment, well beyond the reach of most rental budgets. In Stamford, where average rents for a three-bedroom apartment in desirable areas hover in the high $3,000s to low $4,000s, this home represents a level of exclusivity that is nearly impossible to replicate through leasing.

The continued demand for homes with private club access, deep-water docks, and direct water views reflects a broader trend in Stamford: buyers are prioritizing lifestyle and convenience over pure square footage. Proximity to downtown Stamford, with its transit options, dining, and employment centers, remains a major draw—but so does the ability to step outside and be near water without a long commute. This home’s location near the water, combined with its access to a full-service club, offers a self-contained lifestyle that many professionals and families now seek.

That said, the lack of recent sales in Dolphin Cove suggests that the market for such high-end, private properties is slow-moving. This may indicate either a small pool of qualified buyers or a cautious approach to pricing. For buyers, this could mean room to negotiate, especially if the property remains on the market beyond the typical 30–60 day window. For renters, it’s a reminder that while luxury options exist, they are not accessible through rental markets—especially not in neighborhoods with private amenities and long-term ownership.

Ultimately, this listing is not just about one home. It’s a snapshot of what makes certain parts of Stamford highly desirable: exclusivity, access to nature, and a curated community experience. For those looking to buy or rent in Stamford, it’s a reminder that the most valuable real estate isn’t always the newest—it’s often the one that’s been quietly cherished for generations.
