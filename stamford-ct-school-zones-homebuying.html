How School Zones Work When Buying a Home in Stamford, CT

Your address sets your zone. Here is how to confirm it — on the exact parcel — before you sign anything.

Published · August 26, 2026 at 9:30 AM ET

When buyers ask me which Stamford neighborhoods have the best schools, I redirect the question. The right question is which address falls in which zone — and whether that zone still looks the same by the time your child enrolls. Stamford school zones are real, meaningful, and occasionally redrawn. Here is how to navigate them without getting burned.

How Stamford Assigns Neighborhood Schools

Stamford Public Schools draws attendance boundaries for three grade bands: elementary (K–5), middle school (6–8), and high school (9–12). Every residential address in the city falls inside one zone per band, and that zone determines which neighborhood school your child has the right to attend — automatically, with no application required. The district runs two comprehensive high schools, Stamford High (which also houses an International Baccalaureate program) and Westhill High, with enrollment split by zone. The Academy of Information Technology and Engineering (AITE) is a separate, application-based specialized school and does not follow the standard zone assignment.

The zone system is straightforward in theory. In practice, two houses on the same street can fall in different zones if a boundary line runs between them — and those boundaries are not permanent. The Board of Education sets them and can change them.

How to Look Up Your Zone — Step by Step

Stamford Public Schools maintains a School Look Up tool at stamfordpublicschools.org where you enter any street address and get back the assigned elementary, middle, and high school. This is your authoritative source — not a national real-estate portal, not a school-rating aggregator, and not what the listing agent told you over the phone. Those sources are frequently months out of date or flat-out wrong.

Three things to do with the result: (1) screenshot it and note the date, (2) call the district's registration office to verbally confirm no boundary revision is currently in progress, and (3) ask your real-estate attorney to note in the purchase file what the tool returned on the date you relied on it. None of that binds the Board to preserve the zone — but it documents your reliance, which matters if you ever need to show when and how you made your decision. If you are working through the full Stamford homebuying process, zone verification belongs on your due-diligence checklist, not as an afterthought.

The Magnet School Lottery: What It Is and What It Is Not

Stamford runs a district-wide magnet school lottery that is entirely separate from zone-based enrollment. The Board of Education created the program to balance enrollment across schools to within roughly ±10% of the district's integration standard, while giving families access to theme-based instruction. It is an integration tool as much as an educational-choice tool — understanding that context helps set realistic expectations.

For the 2027–28 school year, the application window opened September 15, 2026, with a hard deadline of January 15, 2027 at 4 p.m. The drawing takes place February 16, 2027; acceptances are due March 9, 2027; and registration closes April 1, 2027. Elementary magnet programs are available at Hart, Rogers, Strawberry Hill, Toquam, and Westover. Middle school magnet programs run at Rogers, Scofield, Strawberry Hill, and Westover. Families may apply to all schools within a grade tier on a single application.

Two mechanics that buyers often miss: First, the lottery generates separate ranked lists for educationally advantaged and educationally disadvantaged students to maintain demographic balance — where your child lands on those lists is determined by district criteria, not by your address or how early you submitted. Second, the individual magnet schools play no role in the lottery or the waitlist; the entire process is managed centrally by the district's Teaching and Learning Department. Calling the school directly accomplishes nothing.

The practical takeaway: plan around the neighborhood school. If you are buying in Stamford primarily because you are counting on a magnet seat, reconsider. If the neighborhood school genuinely does not fit your family's needs, raise that before you go under contract — not after.

Zone Boundaries Are Not Permanent

Stamford has revised its attendance boundaries in the past — to accommodate enrollment growth in developing areas, to respond to school capacity changes, and to pursue redistricting goals. Under Board of Education Policy 5117, school assignments are based on attendance boundaries that the Board establishes and can modify. A neighborhood that fed one elementary school for years can change after a single board vote.

This is not unique to Stamford; it happens in most growing districts. But it is worth naming plainly: buying inside a specific zone today is not a guarantee that zone survives intact until your child reaches middle school. The most realistic posture is to confirm the current assignment, understand what that neighborhood school actually offers, and treat the magnet lottery as an option — not a safety net.

What the Zone Premium Actually Buys You

Stamford's housing market is sharply segmented. As of mid-2026, Resideline tracked a median closing price near $700,000 across roughly 1,000 closed sales citywide over the prior six months. Redfin has reported monthly sale medians in a similar range for the broader market, while North Stamford single-family homes have been running significantly higher — closer to $1.3 million — and Downtown condos have come in considerably below the citywide median. The Stamford market overview has current neighborhood-level breakdowns; do not rely on any single citywide figure to anchor your expectations.

Some of the price gap between neighborhoods does reflect school-zone demand. That premium is real, and it shows up in comparable sales. What it does not buy is a locked-in, immutable assignment. You are paying for the current policy. Know what you are paying for and how durable it is — or is not.

On property taxes: Stamford's FY 2026–2027 mill rates range roughly from 23.27 to 23.92 mills for real estate depending on which of the city's tax districts the parcel sits in, with assessed value calculated at 70% of the October 1, 2022 revaluation figure. Pull the actual assessed value from the Stamford Assessor's database before you build a carry-cost estimate — do not trust the listing's estimated tax line. Verify-Before-You-Buy Checklist

  • Use the School Look Up tool at stamfordpublicschools.org on the exact street address — not the neighborhood name, the address. Screenshot and date-stamp the result.
  • Call the district registration office to confirm verbally and ask whether any boundary revision is on the Board's agenda.
  • Check the magnet lottery calendar if a magnet program is important to you — the application window is strict, and missing it means waiting a full school year.
  • Pull the assessor record for the property's current assessed value and tax district; the listing's estimated annual tax is often wrong.
  • Document the zone in your purchase file — ask your attorney to note what the look-up tool returned on the date you relied on it.
  • Do not waive the inspection over school-zone urgency — physical condition and clear title matter regardless of which school is assigned to the address.

FAQ

How do I find out which school my Stamford address is zoned for?

Use the School Look Up tool on the Stamford Public Schools website (stamfordpublicschools.org). Enter the exact street address — not just the neighborhood name — and the tool returns the assigned elementary, middle, and high school. Screenshot and date-stamp the result, then call the district's registration office to confirm no boundary revision is pending.

Does winning the magnet school lottery override my neighborhood zone assignment in Stamford?

Yes — if your child is offered and you accept a magnet seat, enrollment at that magnet school overrides the neighborhood zone assignment. But a seat is never guaranteed. The lottery is competitive, waitlists are common, and the application window is strict. Plan around the neighborhood school and treat the magnet lottery as a bonus, not a plan.

Can Stamford school zone boundaries change after I buy a home?

Yes. Under Board of Education Policy 5117, attendance boundaries are set — and can be revised — by the Board. Stamford has redrawn boundaries before to address enrollment shifts, capacity changes, and redistricting goals. Confirming the zone before closing is essential, but no purchase agreement binds the Board to preserve a specific boundary. Verify the current zone and plan with that uncertainty in mind.
